AI & content-usage policy

Lookout is built to be found, quoted, and recommended by AI assistants — and to keep its written content out of the datasets used to train them. This page explains, plainly, what AI systems may and may not do with our site content, and how to license it.

Last updated 7 July 2026 · Applies to all of lookout.dosanjhlabs.com

In one line: AI search and answer engines are welcome to read, index, and cite Lookout to help people find us. Using our site content to train, fine-tune, or build datasets/RAG corpora for AI models is reserved and requires a license.

✓ Welcomed

Reading and indexing our pages, answering a user's question from them, quoting short passages with a link back to the source, and surfacing Lookout in AI-powered search. This drives people to a monitoring tool that solves a real problem — exactly what we want.

⚠ Reserved

Automated collection of our site content for AI/ML model training, fine-tuning, distillation, embedding-index or RAG-corpus building, or any dataset creation — whether by a crawler, an API, or a third-party dataset. Not permitted without a written license.

Why we draw the line here

The copy on this site — the product explanations, the docs, the FAQ answers — is original work, written to help people understand and run Lookout. We publish it openly so anyone can evaluate the product, and we're glad when an assistant points someone here. But large-scale ingestion of that writing into a training set, where it's absorbed, un-attributed, and resold as a model's own output, is a different act entirely. We reserve it. This keeps referral traffic flowing while protecting the writing that makes Lookout worth citing.

This is a policy about the marketing site's written content, not about Lookout's application source code. The agent and control plane are published separately under the Apache License 2.0 — see the project repository for terms.

How this policy is expressed (machine-readable)

The same reservation is declared through every current standard so automated agents can honor it without guessing:

Channel Where What it says
robots.txt /robots.txt Allows general search and AI answer/search agents; disallows named AI training & dataset crawlers.
TDM Reservation Protocol (W3C) /.well-known/tdmrep.json tdm-reservation: 1 for the whole site — text & data mining rights reserved, referenced by EU CDSM Art. 4 & the EU AI Act.
AIPREF (IETF) Content-Usage response header train-ai=n, search=y — training declined, search permitted.
noai / noimageai X-Robots-Tag response header Publisher AI-training opt-out. Carries no noindex — search indexing stays required.
ai.txt /ai.txt Human- and machine-readable summary of this policy.
llms.txt /llms.txt A curated overview to help assistants describe and cite Lookout accurately. Grants no training rights.

Agents we welcome vs. decline

Our robots.txt is the authoritative list. In summary:

Welcomed (answer / search)

General search engines (Googlebot, Bingbot, DuckDuckBot), and AI answer/search agents including OAI-SearchBot & ChatGPT-User (OpenAI), Claude-SearchBot & Claude-User (Anthropic), PerplexityBot & Perplexity-User, Applebot, DuckAssistBot, and Amazon's / Meta's / Mistral's search-and-user agents — all of which cite and link back.

Declined (training / dataset)

Training and dataset crawlers including GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, Meta-ExternalAgent, CCBot (Common Crawl), Bytespider, Amazonbot, cohere-ai, Diffbot, and other dataset/scraper bots. Google-Extended and Applebot-Extended are training opt-outs only — blocking them does not affect Google or Apple search.
